VPet Roadmap

Last reviewed: 2026-09-25 (v0.1.0, after the MVP 2 / engineering-health / UX / evolution work). A visual version is in roadmap.html. The full specification is in overview.md §71–72.

Unique selling point

The only desktop pet that actually grows up with you, and is private by design. It hatches knowing nothing, learns words from you, forms its own opinions from real memories, and evolves into a one-of-a-kind creature shaped by how you cared for it. It all runs offline on your PC.

Three things set it apart, and competitors rarely have more than one of them:

  1. Earned intelligence, not a chatbot in a costume. Other AI desktop pets plug a full LLM in from the first minute. VPet's speech is limited by its real language stage (sounds, then words, then sentences), and every belief must cite a real memory. The first time it says your name, it has earned it.
  2. A unique creature, not a skin. The procedural genome, care mistakes, personality and interests drive evolution, and generations pass traits on (Creatures-style artificial life in a Tamagotchi shell). No two pets look or act the same.
  3. Private by architecture, not by policy. The pet runs a local LLM with no account. All network code lives in one gated module that a test enforces, activity observation is opt-in and reads app names only, and one click deletes everything. Most AI competitors need API keys or cloud services.

Short tagline: "Raise it. Teach it. It becomes yours: offline."

Life cycle: 12 stages

Every pet starts as an egg and can live through twelve stages:

  • Egg, Baby and Young keep the look of the egg's animal.
  • Nine evolved forms follow (Rookie to Apex). Each has its own style, chosen in code from the pet's real life: what it saw you do, who it became, and how it was raised.

The pictures below come from the real renderer (npm run docs:images). The art is in the style of monster-raising games (Pokémon, Digimon): hand-painted creatures with a dark outline, cel shading, big eyes and a stepping gait. Baby, Young and the animal-bodied evolved forms are painted sprites (see art/README.md); the transformed bodies and great forms from Champion on are still the older chunky template art, and are being redone.

Each evolution happens when readiness reaches 90%. Readiness weighs:

  • age 35%
  • care 25%
  • familiarity 15%
  • a settled personality 10%
  • goals reached 15%

Every stage lasts at least 24 simulated hours. A well-cared-for pet reaches Apex in about 8 weeks.

How each form is chosen

All of this is decided in code (crates/vpet-core/src/style.rs). The same life always gives the same form, and the language model only names the form and tells its story.

Part Comes from
Gear What it saw you do: music → headphones, coding → goggles, games → cap, videos → scarf, reading → glasses, design → beret, chat → bow tie, office → tie. If activity isn't observed, its personality: brave → bandana, curious → goggles, creative → beret or flower crown, affectionate → bow tie, competitive → cap, cautious → glasses, sociable → scarf. It keeps earlier gear; one piece per place (head, ears, eyes, neck); 1 slot at Rookie, 2 at Champion, 3 at Mega. Apex wears a crown.
Element Its energy genes, your habits and its temperament. Watching videos brings wind to a gentle pet and fire to a fierce one; games and code bring electric; music water; reading and chat nature.
Flight (Adept+) Coders and gamers get a jetpack. Otherwise independence, curiosity or wind give wings: bat if fierce, insect for bug-born pets, feathers otherwise; energy wings from Mega.
Body (Champion) Electric + tech → mech. Fierce + fire or water → serpent. Gentle + wind, nature or water → spirit (floats, so it flies). Brave and not gentle → biped. Otherwise a little of its own randomness decides.
Great forms (Mega, Legend, Mythic, Apex) One of fifteen at each stage, never one it has been, scored from its life: Titan (brave and careful), Celestial and Angel (gentle and loving), Abyss (fierce, independent, or neglected), Dragon (fierce with fire or wings), Mecha (code and games), Beast King (brave and competitive), Spirit and Plant (gentle, water and wind, or nature), Cosmic and Orb (curious, creative, reading, music), Ancient (careful, reading or office work), Human (sociable, chatting), Kaiju (fierce gamers), Ascendant (Apex only, for a well-loved pet). The grandest only come late: Cosmic and Orb from Mythic, Ascendant only at Apex. Each form brings its own colour mix.
Markings (Adept+) and armour (Veteran+) Every stage shows a change: markings of its own at Adept, armour at Veteran.
Robot parts (Adept+) A fierce or competitive pet who codes, games or carries electricity builds one per stage: a robot leg, then an arm, then an eyepiece.
Weapon (Champion+) Fierce and into games or winning: an arm cannon. Gentle and into reading, design or new ideas: a magic staff. Every fierce or gentle pet has one by Ultimate. A four-legged pet's staff floats at its back.
Orb (Ultimate+) Its element gathers into an orb that floats beside it.
Colour Pulled towards the element's colour (40%, or 75% at transform stages); darker if fierce, softer if gentle. Each great form adds its own main and second colour, so the palette becomes a mix.
Its own line A variant from its own seed picks its marking pattern (stripes, spots, zigzags or bands), nudges its second colour and adds a little randomness to every choice. The evolution animation

  1. Charging (while its mind designs the new form, a few seconds to half a minute): it stays put, trembles, a white outline flickers, and motes of light rise.
  2. The transformation (9 seconds):
Charging 0.4 s glow 1.6 s old shape 2.3 s new shape 4.2 s faster 5.0 s burst 6.2 s reveal 7.6 s signature
  • 0–1 s: the old form glows brighter.
  • 1–4.8 s: white silhouettes flash between the old and new shapes, faster and faster, with light rays turning behind.
  • 4.8–5.4 s: a burst of light, with the fanfare.
  • 5.4–9 s: the new form's signature move. Flyers take off and settle; its element bursts outwards; a fierce form stomps; a gentle form sends up hearts; a music lover sends up notes.

Then it tells you, in its own words at its language stage, how and why it grew: "Look at me! I got headphones because I watched you listening to music a lot." The Status window's Growing up section keeps a before-and-after card for every step.

With reduced motion on there is no flashing: the new form simply appears.

VPet

A Windows desktop virtual pet. You pick one of six eggs, and it grows into a creature that exists only on your computer. It runs local-first with no cloud. See overview.md for the full product specification.

Status (see ROADMAP.md):

  • MVP 1 (Living Pet): complete.
  • MVP 2 (Learning Pet): complete: 48×48 colour-V-pet-style pixel art, opt-in activity learning, vocabulary, language stages, local-LLM conversation, Low/Balanced/Performance hardware profiles and optional GPU (Vulkan) acceleration with CPU fallback.
  • MVP 3 (Companion): in progress. Done so far: watching videos and games with you, movie and game reactions (opt-in), the pet's own goals, independent refusals, retro sound effects, a pixel-art UI with light/dark themes, quiet hours and do-not-disturb, reduced motion, and a speed-run mode for testing.
  • MVP 4 (Evolution): mostly done: genome evolution, generations, evolution portraits, and the pet explaining in conversation why it grew, from real data only (spec §44).

Layout

Path What it is
crates/vpet-core Pure, deterministic simulation: needs, life stages, personality, relationship, genome, behavior, events, memory, activity classification, interest learning, vocabulary, language stages and reply planning. The pet aggregate lives in pet/, split by concern. It has no OS, UI, storage or LLM dependencies.
crates/vpet-storage SQLite persistence (rusqlite, bundled) with versioned migrations (schema_v1.sql … schema_v7.sql), plus rolling backups and automatic recovery (backup.rs).
crates/vpet-llm The LanguageModel trait plus llama.cpp linked in through llama-cpp-2. The model loads on first use and unloads when idle. Its optional vulkan feature runs it on the GPU, falling back to the CPU on any GPU failure.
src-tauri Desktop shell: pet window, movement, click-through, menus, tray, the foreground-app sampler (activity_win.rs), the LLM worker thread (brain.rs), hardware profiles (hardware.rs), do-not-disturb and motion (comfort.rs, focus_win.rs), the network gate (netgate.rs) and opt-in updates (updater.rs). runtime/ owns the live pet.
src TypeScript UI. It only renders and plays sounds: the procedural pixel sprite and portraits (renderer/), the pet, status (status/ panels), talk, mind and pixel-menu windows, the shared pixel theme (theme.css, Rainyhearts font), interface text (i18n.ts) and the 8-bit sound synthesizer (sfx.ts).
src-tauri/installer/hooks.nsh Installer hook that downloads the language model while installing.
.github/workflows CI (tests, clippy, UI build, GPU build) and the tagged release (signed installer + update manifest). See docs/RELEASING.md.

The simulation, memory and learning are compiled Rust. The web UI contains no pet logic.

Requirements (development)

  • Rust (stable, MSVC) plus the Visual Studio 2022 C++ Build Tools (these include CMake)
  • LLVM (winget install LLVM.LLVM), because llama.cpp's bindings need libclang. Paths are set in .cargo/config.toml.
  • Node.js 20+
  • Windows Smart App Control must be off on the dev machine.
  • Optional, for the GPU build: the Vulkan SDK (winget install KhronosGroup.VulkanSDK). Build GPU variants through scripts/gpu.ps1, which sets up MSVC, Ninja and a short target folder.
  • Model: Qwen3-4B-Q4_K_M.gguf (2.5 GB). The installer puts it in <install dir>\models\; in development put it in %APPDATA%\com.vpet.desktop\models\. Without it the pet still learns words and speaks simply.

Interacting

  • Six eggs (picked in a pixel egg picker on first run, see eggs.html). Each sets a body plan and a way of moving: Sky flies and hovers above the taskbar, Walker walks, Hopper travels in hops, Aqua swims in waves, Dash runs fast, Shell crawls slowly. The pet's seed varies colour, proportions and starting parts, so no two pets from the same egg look alike.

  • Care mistakes (care.rs): when food, energy or fun drops below 15% the pet calls, and 15 minutes (need-time) without help is a care mistake, like a V-pet. Mistakes lower trust, make it more independent and wary, and push the next evolution to a tougher look (sharp eyes, spikes, duller colours). They reset each stage.
  • Generations (true evolution): 72 simulated hours after reaching Advanced, a healthy pet lays an egg. Hatch the next generation (Status > Pet > Family) retires it to the family tree. The child inherits its egg kind, most of its colour, some of its parts (with mutations) and a quarter of its personality, and knows your name. Settings and learned habits stay.
  • Its own mind (mind.rs, the "Generative Agents" pattern): the pet thinks with the local language model in the background, one thought at a time, on a schedule set by the simulation.
    • Evolution design: when readiness says it's ready, the model designs the new form: a name, a one-line story, up to two new parts and up to three growing/shrinking features, an element and a colour shift, all from the pet's real life. A GBNF grammar forces valid JSON, and the engine validates it (only parts that fit its body plan, same face and main colour, gene-step limits, genome check). No model means code-designed evolution.
    • Reflection: after 4+ new memories (at most every 20 real minutes), it draws up to three beliefs. Each must cite real memory numbers, or it's dropped. Repeated beliefs grow in confidence and unsupported ones fade.
    • Daily plan: once per pet-day it picks a mood (which gently biases behaviour), a goal and three things to say; the lines pass through its language-stage filter.
    • Comments while watching together, after a sudden loud moment.
    • The Mind window shows its beliefs (and the memories behind them), today's plan, its form and what it's thinking about. Talk replies use its beliefs, mood and form.
  • Six egg body plans with archetypes, inspired by how Digimon are built (all original designs): Sky is an ear-wing glider, a bird with beak and crest, or a flying bug (babies float); Walker is a pup, dino or bear cub; Hopper a bunny or frog; Aqua a seal, finned swimmer or tadpole; Dash a fox or lightning pup; Shell a turtle, armadillo or little rock creature. Evolution only grows parts that fit the body plan (no winged cats, no antlered fish).
  • Left-click pets it, drag carries it, and right-click opens a pixel menu: Feed, Play, Talk, What is it thinking?, Status & Settings, and Quit. The menu works with the arrow keys and Escape too.
  • Sound effects (on by default; Status > Settings): 8-bit chirps for petting, eating, playing, asking, refusing, scares, cheering, landing, sleeping, hatching and growing up. Speech bubbles type out with beeps. The pet's beeps start high and get lower as it grows. Everything is synthesised live, with no sound files.
  • Look (Status > Settings): size, opacity, a light or dark-terminal theme (or follow Windows), and "Easy to read" text (pixel font for headings, buttons and speech; clear font for long text) or "Pixel everywhere".
  • Performance (Status > Settings, spec §58): Auto picks a profile from this PC's memory, CPU and graphics card, or choose one yourself:
    • Low: Light model, CPU only, 2k context, thinks a third as often, unloads the model after a minute.
    • Balanced: Full model if there are 8 GB of memory, on the graphics card if it has at least 3 GB of its own.
    • Performance: Full model on the GPU, 8k context, reflects and comments twice as often.
    • The pet always works without a graphics card; a GPU build falls back to the CPU on any GPU error.
  • Quiet & motion (Status > Settings):
    • Quiet while Windows says you're busy (fullscreen app or game, presentation), on by default, and optional quiet hours.
    • While quiet the pet doesn't start conversations or make sounds, and its calls for help wait, so being busy is never a care mistake. It can also hide completely.
    • Movement follows Windows' animation setting or can be set to Calm: the pet stays in place, doesn't hop or fly, and its speech appears at once.
  • First steps: the Pet tab shows what to do while it's an egg or a baby. A warmth meter fills under the egg, and petting a newborn three times teaches its first word, "hi", which it says right away.
  • Accessibility: tabs work with the arrow keys, the pet window describes the pet to screen readers ("Kiba, a young creature. It looks happy."), and speech bubbles are announced.
  • Speed run (Status > Dev, for testing): after typing RESET, it deletes everything and raises a brand-new egg at x10, x60 or x600 speed. The speed survives restarts; Stop returns to real time and keeps that pet.
    • The pet's clock runs fast too, so night comes quickly.
    • Hunger and tiredness run at most x60 (NEED_TIME_SCALE_CAP): at x600 a full tummy lasts about 8 real minutes. A call for help always waits at least one real minute before it becomes a care mistake.
    • Conversations it starts stay at a human pace. Learning from real app use can't be sped up.
    • Test helpers on the same tab: Pretend I'm watching (a video or game) and Evolve now.

    • Egg, Baby and Young keep the egg's animal. Every evolved form has its own style (style.rs), chosen in code from its real life:
      • Gear from what it saw you do: music → headphones, coding → goggles, games → cap, videos → scarf, reading → glasses, design → beret, chat → bow tie, office → tie. Pets whose activity isn't observed get gear from their personality instead. The Apex form is crowned.
      • An element (fire, water, wind, electric or nature) shown as a living aura. Watching videos (anime!) brings wind to a gentle pet and fire to a fierce one.
      • Flight from Adept on: wings (feather, bat, insect, then energy wings at Mega), or a jetpack for coders and gamers. Any animal can learn to fly, even a fox.
      • Robot parts, weapons and orbs: a fierce tech lover builds a robot leg, arm and eyepiece; from Champion a fierce gamer gets an arm cannon and a gentle, bookish pet a magic staff; from Ultimate its element gathers into an orb. Cannons fire, staffs cast rune circles and orbs circle the pet whenever it plays or cheers.
      • Great forms: Mega, Legend, Mythic and Apex each take a new form of fifteen (Titan, Celestial, Abyss, Dragon, Mecha, Beast King, Spirit, Cosmic, Ancient, Ascendant, Human, Angel, Kaiju, Plant, Orb), chosen from its life and never repeated, each with its own colour mix and something alive of its own (a halo, stars, void mist, horns, moons).

      • A new body at the transform stages (Champion, Mega, Apex): biped, mech, dragon-like serpent or floating spirit. It always wears its animal's own head, so the face and ears never change.
      • Body colour moves towards its element's colour, darker for a fierce pet and softer for a gentle one. The eye colour it hatched with never changes.

Then a 9-second transformation plays: the old form glows, white silhouettes flash between the old and new shape faster and faster, there's a burst of light, and the new form does its signature move (flyers take off, its element bursts out, fierce forms stomp, gentle ones send up hearts, music lovers send up notes). With reduced motion on, the new form simply appears.
    • Evolving writes a new genome generation. Growing up makes it bigger and longer-limbed; personality still nudges horns, claws, tail, ears and glow. No gene moves more than 0.15 per evolution, and invalid genomes are softened and retried, never saved.
    • Preview every animal, body, gear piece and frame: npx rolldown scripts/preview-art.ts --platform node --format esm --file target/preview-art.mjs && node target/preview-art.mjs [animals|painted|parts|bodies|forms] (see art/README.md).
    • Saves from before the twelve stages load as the stage they became (developing → Young, young → Rookie, mature → Champion, advanced → Ultimate).
    • Each evolution is remembered ("I evolved into a young creature. My tail got longer…").
    • Portraits: Status > Pet > Growing up shows every step as a card with the pet before and after, its form's name and story, and why it changed.
    • It explains itself (spec §44): every visible change is matched to a real cause in code: its personality ("Horns grew because I've grown brave."), its care ("…because nobody came when I called, so I got tougher"), or what it has seen you do often ("…because I watched you playing games a lot").       • Right after evolving, it tells you in its bubble.
      • Ask it "Why did you evolve?" or "How did you get those horns?": a fluent pet (stage 5) answers straight from the record, with no model involved. Younger speakers get the same reasons in their prompt and are told never to give any other cause.

    • It starts conversations by itself every few minutes when you're near or it's lonely, and sometimes adds a follow-up question to its replies.
    • Your answer teaches it: a name or an explained word is learned at once, and answers become memories it can talk about later.
  • No repetition: replies drop sentences the pet said in its last few messages, the model runs with a repetition penalty, and the pet never makes up a name for itself.
  • What is it thinking? shows a live graph of the pet's real decision:
    • its needs, traits, bond and goal on the left;
    • the options it is weighing, sized by urge, in the middle;
    • its current action and what it has learned on the right;
    • a thought stream built from real events.
  • Goals: the pet forms its own goals from its personality, e.g. explore more, spend time with you, learn a word, or watch together. Reaching one becomes a memory and strengthens the trait behind it. A pet busy with its own goal may refuse to play (spec §20).

Stage How it talks Unlocked by
0 Nothing (egg) —
1 Sounds, or echoing a word it already knows Hatching
2 Single words it knows 3 learned words
3 1–4 known words (LLM, filtered to its vocabulary) 20 words, 5 conversations, 24 h old
4 Simple sentences, 12 words max, using known words plus words you just said 60 words, 20 conversations, developing stage
5 Natural speech, 40 words max 150 words, 50 conversations, young stage

  • The LLM receives only real state: needs, emerging personality, bond, hedged interests, and the most relevant real memories. It's told these are the only things it remembers.

Data and privacy

  • Fully offline by default.
    • All network code lives in src-tauri/src/netgate.rs, and a test fails the build if it appears anywhere else (sub-folders included).
    • The gate refuses every request unless you turn on Internet access in Privacy settings. Even then it only starts requests at allow-listed HTTPS hosts (the model download and, if you ask, the update check), follows redirects only to those services' own file servers, and stops mid-transfer if you turn access off.
    • The web UI is locked down by CSP and a dead proxy, so it can never reach the internet.
  • Updates are opt-in (Privacy > Updates): press Check now, or turn on a daily check. Both still need Internet access. An update installs only if its signature verifies against the key built into the app; see docs/RELEASING.md.
  • All data is stored in %APPDATA%\com.vpet.desktop\vpet.db. Nothing leaves the machine.
  • Crash safety:
    • The database commits with synchronous = FULL, and milestones (hatching, evolving, naming) are saved the moment they happen.
    • A consistent backup is written at startup and every 6 hours, keeping two (vpet.db.bak1, vpet.db.bak2).
    • If the save file is ever damaged, it is set aside (not deleted), the newest good backup is restored, and the Status window says so.
  • Activity observation is off until you allow it. After hatching, the status window asks once.
  • When on, observation samples every 5 seconds: the foreground app's exe name (for known apps only; others are stored as "unknown"), a fullscreen flag and your idle time. It never reads window titles, screen contents or typing. Raw sessions are kept for 30 days; only the learned interests persist beyond that.
  • Without observation, the pet still reads four facts from Windows, none of them stored:
    • whether the left or right mouse button is pressed while the cursor is over the pet (so clicks and drags on it work whatever window has focus; keys are never read);
    • how long since your last key or mouse input, as a number (for rest reminders);
    • whether the desktop or another window has focus (so it keeps to a corner while you work; the app is not identified);
    • whether a fullscreen app or presentation is running (for quiet mode).
  • Rest reminders (on by default, adjustable or off in Quiet & motion): a soft chime and a word from the pet after 50 minutes without a 5-minute break, and once a day after 6 hours of screen time. They wait during quiet time.
  • Sound reactions (off by default) read only the speakers' peak volume level while you watch or play. Audio is never recorded, and the microphone is never used.
  • Screen sharing (opt-in, per session, via Share a screen…): you pick a screen/window/tab in the system picker, and every 15 s one frame is read with Windows' built-in offline OCR (ocr_win.rs). Frames are never stored. Only ordinary all-lower-case words of 3–12 letters are kept; names, numbers, links, e-mail addresses and codes are dropped (screen.rs). A word sticks after three looks. A clear guess of the activity from the text feeds interest learning.
  • Language model size: Light (Qwen3-0.6B, 0.6 GB) or Full (Qwen3-4B, 2.5 GB), switchable in Privacy settings without restarting.
  • Installing downloads the language model (the installer asks: full 2.5 GB, or light 0.6 GB by default) and shows "Downloading resources" in the installer. That one download is the only network use outside the Internet access switch, and it happens in the installer, not the running app. If it fails, the pet works without the model and it can be downloaded later from Privacy settings. Uninstalling removes it.
  • The user's name for the pet is learned like any other word: stored when given, used only once heard 3 times.
  • Delete All Pet Data removes the pet, memories, vocabulary, interests, activity, conversations, settings, backups and any damaged copies, which turns activity, sound reactions, update checks and internet access off again (sound effects go back on; performance goes back to Auto). The model file isn't pet data and stays.
  • The uninstaller's "Delete the application data" checkbox is off by default.

Design rules (from the spec)

  • Needs, timers, movement, stats and learning are deterministic. The LLM never runs them.
  • One experience can change a personality trait by at most 0.01.
  • One observation never defines the user. Interests move from observation → assumption → pattern → preference/habit, and each step needs more hours and more distinct days.
  • Memories come only from real events. The pet talks about its impressions of you with uncertainty ("You seem to like…").
  • Offline decay is capped at 8 hours and the pet never dies. The egg never hatches while the app is closed.

Roadmap

  • MVP 3 remaining: speech input (offline speech-to-text), LLM memory summarization, and a richer daily routine.
  • MVP 5: advanced learning, more complex personality, optional AI art.
  • Localization: see docs/LOCALIZATION.md.

Releasing VPet

Releases are built by .github/workflows/release.yml when a v* tag is pushed. The workflow builds the GPU-enabled NSIS installer, signs it (when the keys below are set up), and publishes a GitHub release with the installer and latest.json for the in-app updater.

Cutting a release

  1. Bump the version in src-tauri/tauri.conf.json, package.json and Cargo.toml ([workspace.package]). The workflow refuses a tag that doesn't match tauri.conf.json.
  2. Make sure CI is green on main.
  3. Tag and push: git tag v0.2.0 && git push origin v0.2.0.
  4. Before announcing it, install the release on:
    • a PC with a dedicated GPU. Status > Privacy should say the model runs "on the graphics card" while talking.
    • a PC or VM with no GPU driver. VPet must start and talk on the CPU. This is the check that the bundled vulkan-1.dll works; CI cannot test it.

One-time setup

1. Code signing (removes the SmartScreen warning)

An unsigned installer shows "Windows protected your PC", which drives users away. Signing needs a certificate that only the publisher can buy:

  • Azure Trusted Signing is the cheapest option for individuals and small companies, and it builds SmartScreen reputation quickly. Use it through Tauri's bundle.windows.signCommand, which is different from the .pfx flow below.
  • An OV or EV code-signing certificate from a CA (DigiCert, Sectigo, SSL.com…). Export it as a .pfx, then add these repository secrets:
    • WINDOWS_CERTIFICATE: base64 of the .pfx (PowerShell: [Convert]::ToBase64String([IO.File]::ReadAllBytes("cert.pfx")))
    • WINDOWS_CERTIFICATE_PASSWORD

The workflow imports the certificate and passes its thumbprint to Tauri, which signs both vpet.exe and the installer and timestamps them with DigiCert's server.

2. Update signing (lets the in-app updater install releases)

The app installs an update only if its signature verifies against the public key built into the app. This protects users even if the release page or download is tampered with.

  1. npx tauri signer generate -w vpet-update.key. Keep the private key and its password somewhere safe, outside the repo. If you lose it, installed copies can no longer update themselves.
  2. Repository secrets: TAURI_SIGNING_PRIVATE_KEY (contents of vpet-update.key) and TAURI_SIGNING_PRIVATE_KEY_PASSWORD.
  3. Repository variable (not secret): VPET_UPDATE_PUBKEY = contents of vpet-update.key.pub. It is compiled into the app (updater.rs).

Without these, releases still build, but the app only reports new versions and asks the user to download them.

3. How updates reach users

  • Nothing is checked unless the user turns on Check for updates (Privacy tab) or presses Check now, and Internet access is allowed. All traffic goes through netgate.rs: only github.com, with redirects allowed only to *.githubusercontent.com.
  • latest.json comes from https://github.com/Yewaung/vpet/releases/latest/download/latest.json (updater::MANIFEST_URL). If the repository moves, update that constant.
  • Installing runs the downloaded installer with /P /R /UPDATE: a progress bar only, an in-place upgrade, and VPet restarts afterwards.

GPU builds locally

Install the Vulkan SDK once (winget install KhronosGroup.VulkanSDK), then run GPU builds through scripts/gpu.ps1:

.\scripts\gpu.ps1 cargo build -p vpet --features gpu
.\scripts\gpu.ps1 npm run tauri dev -- --features gpu
.\scripts\gpu.ps1 cargo test -p vpet-llm --features vulkan

A plain cargo build --features gpu fails. llama.cpp's Vulkan backend builds a helper tool (vulkan-shaders-gen) as a nested CMake project, and that project needs three things:

  • the MSVC developer environment;
  • the Ninja generator, because with the Visual Studio generator MSBuild runs its configure and install steps at the same time and fails;
  • a short target folder (C:\vt), because otherwise its paths pass Windows' 260-character limit.

The script sets all three, and CI and the release workflow do the same.

Localization plan

VPet's interface and its pet are English-only today. This page records what is ready, what is English-specific, and the order to fix it in.

Ready

  • src/i18n.ts: t(key, vars) looks up interface text by key in the language Windows prefers (navigator.languages) and falls back to English. To add a language, add a catalog to CATALOGS.
  • New interface text uses it: Growing up, first-steps tips, Performance, Quiet & motion, Updates, and the pet's screen-reader description.

English-specific (in order of impact)

  1. Older interface text is still written inline in status.ts, mind.ts, talk.ts, menu.ts, eggs.ts, share.ts and the tray menu (lib.rs). Move it into i18n.ts one window at a time.
  2. The pet's own words, all in vpet-core:
    • vocabulary.rs tokenize keeps ASCII letters only, so words with accents or in other scripts are never learned.
    • vocabulary.rs function words (is_function_word) and language.rs shape_reply (stage filters, the "my name is" check, SOUNDS) assume English.
    • curiosity.rs question templates ("What is pizza?") and language::voice_intention phrases.
    • Event thoughts (events.rs), memories (memory.rs) and evolution text (evolution.rs describe/reasons) are English sentences stored in the database. Store keys plus values instead, and render them per language.
    • Care words taught to babies ("food", "play", "hi", "sleep") need a per-language set.
  3. Screen sharing (screen.rs) keeps lower-case a–z words of 3–12 letters. The privacy rule (drop names, numbers, links) must be restated per script; for example, languages without capital letters can't use "lower-case means not a name".
  4. Prompts (language.rs, mind.rs) tell the model to speak English and shape_reply drops other scripts. The Qwen3 models are multilingual, so this is a rule change, not a model change.

Rules that must hold in every language

  • The pet only uses words it has really learned, at its real language stage. Translation must never let it skip stages.
  • Evolution reasons stay code-derived facts (spec §44), translated from keys and never generated freely.
